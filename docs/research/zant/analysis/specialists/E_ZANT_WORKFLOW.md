## What the cast makes concrete

1. Specificity is often in a restrained combination. C020's dark coat/skirt and bright red running shoes are immediately distinctive without piling every visual signal onto every garment. C001's bald head, slightly uneven worn sneakers and blue tracksuit differ from C033's short hair, purple jersey, pale loose denim and high tops despite both being economical casual outfits. Preserve their proportions and styling rather than swapping only an RGB color.
2. Pattern/material is part of identity presentation. C003's burgundy shiny jacket/leopard lace camisole, C018's leopard jacket/red flare trousers and C042's striped leather jacket/floral dress/torn tights are different intentional combinations. Converting them to the same generic top/bottom preset would destroy visible design decisions.
3. Build and age cues are varied in the contact sheet: C019's broad silhouette, C034's belly and dropped casual shirt, C020's face/hair, C033's lean silhouette and C007's cardigan/blouse proportions must not be normalized toward a heroic mannequin. These are visible proportions and styling, not evidence of disposition.
4. Body and face performance complement the outfit. C020 original pairs a flowers-carrying pose and visible finger gesture with a hands-free wrist/hand presentation; cloth drape, shoulders and facial gaze change together. C033 pairs phone/flowers with a broad open-hand gesture and forward lean. C040 pairs held crowbar with another pose that cups a hand near the ear and raises an open palm. These are deliberately authored physical performances, not neutral limbs rotated at joints.
5. Props contribute but must be removable. C006's folder bundle, bag, lifted shoulders, angled body and sideward gaze are a strong signature image; they must not be the only readable anatomy source. C040's held tool cannot supply a missing hand; C033's bouquet cannot replace underarm/torso continuation. Capture signature presentation separately from a genuinely hands-free standard state.
6. Apparent social presentation can be discussed as a design question, but an original image cannot silently supply canonical biography. Neither protective equipment nor formal dress establishes occupation, competence, danger or morality. Do not encode demeanor lens names as gameplay classes or official emotion labels.

## Recommended authoring order

Approve a specific person and their literal visual invariants before searching the reusable library. Then choose technical drafting references, author all four views aligned from inception, separate anatomy/clothing/gear, author a small useful performance set and identity-specific neutral face plus registered slot states, paint material masks alongside garments, and preserve approved source references. Use existing parts only when they serve this person's intended cut, fit and visible choices.

A reused family should save drawing effort without dictating the person. A new cut or hair silhouette can become a candidate reusable family, but starts with explicit fit/view/provenance/QA status. A second compatible host must pass fit and identity review before claiming cross-character interchangeability. Character-specific traits remain allowed and need not be turned into universal variation sliders.

## Bounded production budget for a future ordinary character

The current C001 proof budget is controlled by deliverable05. For a later ordinary addition, approve an explicit asset checklist before drawing: one identity, one calibrated body envelope, four neutral references, one primary outfit with at most one removable outer layer, two useful arm states per anatomical side/view, two modest leg/stance states with recorded allowed pairings, one pair of shoes, one four-view head package, a small face set, two editable materials, and one signature presentation. Existing compatible states can satisfy checklist entries; count genuinely new states separately. Long coats, skirts, extreme jaw silhouettes, complex gear and second fits are additional approved scope rather than hidden expected free work.

At the face level, a practical starting budget is two authored complete performance sets for front and 3/4, one additional coherent gaze pair per expressive view, and one asymmetric recombination using those states; side uses only visible semantics and back none. Those are performance coverage examples, not a mandatory emotion list. Neutral may live in the immutable head or supplied neutral slot set, but its behavior must be unambiguous. This yields at most 24 front/3/4 slot variants if all five slots are represented for the two performance sets plus the extra eye pairs; side and special silhouette states are separately counted. Start smaller where coverage can be convincingly satisfied by fewer new patches.

This bounded library avoids painting every possible combination as a full-body sprite. It still pays the real cost of view/pose-dependent silhouettes and recognizes that a garment family may contain several layers for a single logical state.

## ZANT acceptance gate

Review the person in their intended original look first, then in selected permissible combinations. Ask whether their head shape, posture, proportions, asymmetry, grooming, wear, signature garment decision and gesture economy still read as one intentional individual. A technically valid recipe fails if it looks like a mannequin wearing random attractive options. Do not infer psychology from physical features to make the result feel coherent; the visual author can deliberately supply the relevant visible choices.

Keep literal observation, owner-confirmed intent, authored choices and unknowns distinct. Library metadata describes visible assets and compatibility. Encounter and Room Creator remain read-only consumers; no mappings from facial configuration or body style to social/gameplay semantics are introduced.
