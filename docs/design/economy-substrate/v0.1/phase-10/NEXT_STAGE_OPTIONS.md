The recommendation favors a small concrete question over automatically proceeding to implementation. A paper fixture can expose what an actor would know and what result remains unresolved, but cannot demonstrate actual interface comprehension or executable repeat prevention. If the owner instead wants playable evidence, that is a different separately scoped work order with actual actor-permitted interaction and observation; it should name its question and success limits without claiming full-game balance or fun from one scene.

A bounded executable proof work order can be drafted as a proposal if the owner selects that route, names the consumer/scope and identifies its exact D03/D10 and duty/authority/outcome dependencies. Draftability is not implementation readiness: unresolved guarantees must become explicit prerequisites or bounded questions, and the owner must separately authorize any implementation. This packaging does not draft or begin that order.

The [known limits](KNOWN_LIMITS_FUTURE_PROOF_REQUIREMENTS.md) identify why future runtime, compaction, aggregation, sealing and experience claims need additional evidence. The [evidence summary](AUDIT_VERIFICATION_EVIDENCE_SUMMARY.md) establishes what has already passed at documentary scope. The owner need not resolve all D01–D11 cards to accept the substrate or select a narrow next question; each dependent result must either receive its exact basis or remain unresolved.
