## Documentary cases and expected distinctions

**Stolen goods reenter trade (S-C01).** An existing shipment loses custody of some goods; those goods later appear in a reseller's stock. No second supply arrival is inferred. A subsequent customer payment is a new value movement, not a restoration of the original seller's loss. A commercial receipt may describe the resale while omitting earlier custody history. Resale neither settles title nor teaches the customer its source. Laws classifies the relevant facts separately.

**Specialist fence capacity (S-C02).** An electronics reseller may have interest in electronics and no use for unrelated bulk goods. Even suitable goods can exceed available money, space or expected sales. An offer can be refused or partly accepted without destroying the unsold goods. Owner-authored content may permit other receivers: S-01 cautions against forcing every theft through one preexisting fence order. No acceptance probability, price schedule or criminal method is selected.

**Loss, insurance and later recovery (S-C08).** Goods leave the original holder; the holder reports a loss; an insurer pays under separately established terms; some goods return later. Preserve all four facts and any false descriptions independently. Recovery is not a time reversal or an automatic second payout. Who receives recovered property, whether repayment is owed and how competing interests resolve remain P1-03/04/12 questions. Actors need eligible evidence before reacting.

**Front with ordinary and hidden records (S-C04/05/06).** One business sells legitimate services and makes an additional actual payment to someone whose work is misreported. Official and private accounts can disagree with the occurrence and with each other. An off-books account is neither mandatory physical evidence nor canonical truth. Destroying it affects discoverability, not historical occurrence. Hidden interests do not confer universal employee knowledge. Moving money into an account creates no provenance-erasing state transition.

**Service vice without goods inventory (S-C07).** An abstract adult service venue provides an activity and receives payment; merchandise stock need not change. A gambling activity additionally needs an authoritative outcome and supported stake/payout effects, which this specialist does not resolve. No inferred sale creates money, no promise substitutes for performance, and no generic vice label asserts consent, legality, injury or knowledge. Content boundaries remain owner decisions.

These are reasoning cases, not executed scenarios or audit verdicts. Background aggregates may retain coarse totals without individual customers. Consequential explicit cases must reconcile with already-counted activity; they cannot invent precise past participants, transactions or witnesses. Trace needs do not authorize unlimited C1 history.
