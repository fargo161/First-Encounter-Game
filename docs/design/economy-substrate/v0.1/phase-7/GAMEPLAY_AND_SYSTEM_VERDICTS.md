## A finite counterexample to overclaiming balanced crime

The following is **stipulated analytical arithmetic, NOT RUN**. It intentionally uses compatible but potentially undesirable content; these are not recommended prices, enacted policies or actual gameplay results. One six-hour episode has an available legal shift paying4 from an employer with10. A separately supplied admitted diversion of one existing item takes ten minutes; a willing reseller with10 and suitable capacity pays8 for it. Both routes face the same selected recovery expense3. Classification of diversion as prohibited is supplied by a fictional external rule; success/violence/detection is not resolved by this audit.

Under those assumptions, legal take-home after recovery is4-3=1; diversion/resale take-home is8-3=5 and leaves more of the stated time available. The reseller's balance falls to2; the goods are original stock now held by the reseller. Supported source persists. No future police/injury cost is available in this fixture. Every transfer can be funded and once-counted while the shadow route offers the higher stipulated material return. That falsifies an inference that common primitives plus a finite fence guarantee competitive wages. It does not prove crime universally dominates other content, social opportunities, risk or goals.

The reverse is equally representable: the fence can refuse or pay little, while employment offers better funded returns or valuable acquired interactions. No universal optimal route follows from the architecture. Minimum needed for a broader claim is one common finite horizon and actual present input/time/access/funding/opportunity constraints, with owner-selected progression aims. That requirement is B-N05, not a new mandate for a police subsystem or price formula.
