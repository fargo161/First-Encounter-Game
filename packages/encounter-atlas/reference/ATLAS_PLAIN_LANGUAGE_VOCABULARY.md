# Atlas plain-language vocabulary

Audit date: 2026-10-18 (America/New_York). Design specification; no Atlas implementation.

**Main finding:** Atlas can hide most storage and processing vocabulary. It must keep visible the difference between what happened, what someone received, and what an NPC concludes. “Knows” is not a safe replacement for all three.

## The small vocabulary the developer needs first

Use these 18 concepts as the normal entry point: **person, thing, place, group, event, scenario, owner, current holder, received information, reported claim, belief, suspicion, source, permission, proposed handover, completed result, visit warning/repair, changes in this run**. Group may be absent from a particular scenario.

A name does not identify a person by itself. A person can know a topic exists without knowing its contents. Someone can receive a report without accepting it. The human player's received information is recorded; their private beliefs or feelings are not simulated.

## Detailed translation specification

The tables combine recommended wording, normal developer phrasing and concrete examples. “Hide?” refers to the **technical term**, not to hiding its meaning. Classifications are: **Understand**, **Rename**, **Hide**, **Debug**, **Misleading**, **Unresolved**, or **Legacy/design-only**. All implementation meanings in the tables are verified unless explicitly qualified.

## A. Terms Atlas should completely hide in normal use

Hide storage/adapter terms: canonical serialization, sealed package, digest, schema discriminator, registry, binding token, `sourceRefs`, `fromRef`, `toRef`, `linkRefs`, `scenarioRefs`, `observerId`, `inputBasis`, `expectedRevision`, command envelope, receipt payload, journal, staged resolver, immutable/frozen copy, idempotence, cursor, and internal BASED codes.

## C. Terms and distinctions Atlas should retain

Keep **owner**, **current holder**, **permission**, **report**, **belief**, **suspicion/concern**, **source**, **proposed terms**, **confirmation**, **performed result**, **warning**, and **current versus earlier** readily visible. ## E. Legacy, broader-design and inconsistent terminology

1. **The fifteen ledger verbs are broader design vocabulary, not a live universal ledger.** Master §2.7 names HOLDS, OWNS, OWES, PROMISED_TO, MEMBER_OF, ALLOWED, SOFT, SORE, TRUSTS, FEARS, NEEDS, AIMS_FOR, BELIEVES, IS and HAPPENED; PERCEIVED is additionally first-class in the design. Current M3/AR0 implements bounded custody/ownership, permissions, information/inference, actions/events and descriptive membership. It does not expose working generalized debt, promise, need, goal, affection, resentment, trust or fear systems. SOFT/SORE/TRUSTS/FEARS would eventually need separate ordinary labels, never one “relationship score.” Do not add empty meters now.
2. **M2 shapes remain in shared modules.** Shared `validateClaim` expects a proposition; M3 creates claims with evidence arrays. Shared permission status values include unset/forbidden; M3 permissions use allowed/suspended and different fields. M3 types/engine and current fixtures govern this audit, not similarly named earlier validators. Shared run/command stay `@2`; package/message/domain/save are `@3`; observation remains `@2`; catalog/projection are `@1`. These mixed versions are intentional composition, not one migration target.
3. **Master behavioral ambition exceeds current bounded selection.** Master §3.4 describes broader weighting by needs, relationships, state and framing. Current `chooseResponse` gates role/local information and sorts fixed candidate priorities. Authored profile priorities are present but not used for that ranking. The `acceptanceThreshold` is used for beliefs. Do not describe profile weights as active behavioral controls.
4. **BASED catalog versus authored expressions:** twenty named styles exist in shared constants, but M3 authors only three. Full 20 × 6 × 3 coverage remains outside this checkpoint. Older incoming-vibe reaction tables and inherited conversational trust/reset rules are superseded in Master v2.1 and do not describe current policy.
5. **Atlas versus Assembler versus player notebook:** earlier material discusses a future authoring workbench and a possible player notebook. This audit defines developer inspection. It does not authorize live authoring, apply player visibility to the full developer tool, or specify a journal product.
6. **Event/receipt/alias/revision/knowledge are overloaded.** Use qualified language from the tables. The most hazardous status is an offer marked `accepted` while still awaiting exact confirmation.
7. **“Recent meaningful changes” is selective.** It must not be advertised as all history or a complete before/after diff. Complete causal inspection sometimes requires the run journal/engine inspector.
8. **Spatial/time ambiguity remains:** Office storage is the current holder before transfer, while evidence says Dry storage. These are distinct Places without an explicit containment link. Fact records lack a temporal scope field, and dry-storage evidence persists after handover. Display the supported fields separately; do not resolve the gap by inventing a current-location model.
9. **Readiness headings have historical scope.** Root README/package metadata/test report still describe M3. R1 START_HERE identifies older AR0 reports as historical. The external independent R1 verdict supersedes “independent review pending”; `before-*` projections deliberately show the old identity defect, not current behavior.

## F. Atlas language style

- Name the holder and basis: “Orrin reported …,” “Tamsin believes …,” “Current holder: Collector.” Use “Known” only for a clearly defined information grouping, never as a truth assertion.
- Use short verbs when correct: **Owns, Holds, Read, Heard, Was told, Believes, Suspects, Reported, Changed, Happened**. Use **Received starting information** when a sensory verb would invent perception.
- Describe record absence accurately: **No received information recorded in this run**, **Not shown in this view**, or **This person has no modeled local state**. Do not turn missing data into a negative fact.
- Separate current state, earlier evidence and interpretation. Include **Earlier**, **At this step**, **According to**, or **Awaiting confirmation** where needed.
- Preserve actual scope and qualifiers: **unidentified wrapped case**, **case-handling extract**, **protected contact information**. Avoid shortening a fragment into a stronger claim.
- Use names first; show identity, full field names and raw values under **Encounter details**. If ambiguity affects the answer, expose it immediately.
- Show **Source** beside an assertion. Expanding it should reveal the immediate receipt/report and earlier citations, keeping full developer source inspection separate from a character's received fragment.
- State the run, content/catalog version and view somewhere persistent in the future tool. Use step/order rather than invented dates.
- Unknown relation/form/kind should display **Untranslated Encounter detail** with the raw value. Do not guess a friendly meaning or silently drop it. This is a future-reader requirement, not an implementation made here.

**Recommendation on progressive disclosure:** it fits the detached cards/links/source references well. Normal summaries can be short, while the original records remain inspectable. The basis, holder, uncertainty, scope and pending/completed status must remain visible in the summary; those distinctions cannot all be deferred to a technical drawer.
